# A year's astronomy, worked out once

**Status:** done, 2026-10-26, in Calendrical 2.1.0 on Astro 2.9.0: a per-year cache in ETS, not tables. See [Decision](#decision).

Calendrical 2.0.0 runs on Astro 2.8, which computes solar and lunar positions from the JPL DE440s ephemeris, corrected for light time and aberration, where Astro 2.7 used the Meeus series. A position costs more, and every calendar that finds its years or months by astronomy works them out again on each call and keeps nothing, so the cost is paid on every call.

## Findings

Measured on Calendrical `95d867d` with Astro 2.8.0 and Localize 1.4.0, Elixir 1.20.4 / OTP 29, load average under 10:

| Call | Time | JPL positions a call |
|---|---|---|
| `Calendrical.Persian.date_to_iso_days/3` | 108 µs | 16 (the Tehran equinox and its solar noon) |
| `Calendrical.Persian.date_from_iso_days/1` | 448 µs | up to six new years |
| `Calendrical.Chinese.days_in_month/2` | 660 µs | 69 (solar terms, about 22 new-moon searches) |
| `Calendrical.Chinese.date_to_iso_days/3`, and Korean, Vietnamese, LunarJapanese | 254–281 µs | |
| `Calendrical.Islamic.Observational.date_to_iso_days/3` | 17.9 ms | about 2,600 (moonset and sunset found day by day) |
| `Calendrical.Islamic.Rgsa.date_from_iso_days/1` | 23.7 ms | |
| `Calendrical.Islamic.UmmAlQura.Astronomical.first_day_of_month/2` | 7.8 ms | |
| `Calendrical.Islamic.UmmAlQura` (KACST's table), `Calendrical.Gregorian` | 0.4–0.5 µs | none |

Tempo measured the first two on Astro 2.7 at 21 and 102 µs. Astro's own primitives cost 8 µs for an equinox, 16 µs for a solar longitude and 22 µs for a new moon, so the cost is in how often the calendars ask, not in any one answer. `valid_date?/3` and `date_to_iso_days/3` each work the year out too, so a validated conversion pays twice (the lunar-year item deferred on 2026-10-11).

Working out a whole year once costs about 1 ms for the Persian calendar, 3.7 to 3.9 ms for each lunisolar calendar, 80 ms for the astronomical Umm al-Qura calendar and 220 ms for each observational Islamic calendar. Over the ephemeris span, Gregorian 1900 to 2100, that is 0.2 s, 0.7 to 0.8 s each, 16 s, and 44 s each.

## Options

* **On first access, into `:persistent_term`.** One put a calendar, as `Calendrical.Month` and `Calendrical.Era` keep theirs. Workable for the Persian and lunisolar calendars (a first call stalls for under a second), not for the observational Islamic ones (44 s). Answers still depend on the ephemeris installed.

* **At compile time, in the generated module.** Needs the ephemeris loaded while compiling and adds about 3 s for the lunisolar calendars and minutes for the observational ones to every build of Calendrical.

* **A year's results kept in ETS as they are asked for.** A table owned by Calendrical's application, read concurrently: every year's first call pays as now, every later call costs a lookup. Nothing to regenerate; the first touch of each year stays slow.

* **Tables shipped in `priv`, generated by a mix task.** As `Calendrical.Islamic.UmmAlQura` carries KACST's month lengths: the Persian new year, and each lunisolar and observational calendar's month starts and leap month, for the years of the ephemeris span, compiled into module attributes and looked up in under a microsecond. Answers in the span no longer depend on the ephemeris a user installs, nor change with Astro; the task is rerun when Astro's positions change, and a `:full` test compares the table with the astronomy. Years outside the span are worked out as now.

## Recommendation

Ship tables in `priv` for the six calendars, generated by one mix task over Gregorian 1900 to 2100, and keep the astronomy for years outside them. It is the only option that serves the observational Islamic calendars, it makes `valid_date?/3` and `date_to_iso_days/3` both a lookup, which answers the deferred lunar-year item without joining the two questions, and it makes the span's answers independent of the installation. An ETS memo for years outside the span can follow if they prove slow in use.

## Decision

The user began the tables (step 1, the generator and the Persian table) and then set them aside: "I'm uncomfortable this is in Calendrical. Shouldn't it go in Astro instead?", then "Hold Calendrical, do the fix in Astro now", and once Astro was as fast as it could be made with the same results, "now add a per-year cache in Calendrical. I will not publish Astro 2.8.1 until we are certain we have best optimized Calendrical." Step 1 is kept as [astronomy-tables-step1.patch](astronomy-tables-step1.patch), which applies to `95d867d`.

### Astro 2.9.0 (published 2026-10-26)

Faster with results unchanged to the last bit: a coverage check by segment lookup, the nutation computed once for an ecliptic position, and rise and set scans that stop at the first bracket. A JPL position cannot approach the Meeus series' 1.2 µs, and Calendrical gained 10 to 45 %. A reuse of each segment's last decoded Chebyshev record, kept in the calling process's dictionary, was tried and removed: the process dictionary is never used (user, 2026-10-26).

### The cache

`Calendrical.YearCache` is an ETS table owned by a process `Calendrical.Supervisor` starts. Each entry is a pure function of its key, kept once worked out:

* `Calendrical.Persian`: an astronomical year's new year.
* The lunisolar calendars: a year's new year, its suis and its new year and leap month, keyed by the calendar's place and the year's mean middle (`mid_year/3`, through which alone they depend on the year and the epoch); a month's start, keyed by its new year and month; a solar term.
* `Calendrical.Islamic.Observational` and `Rgsa`: a month's first day, and the crescent searches it is found by.
* `Calendrical.Islamic.UmmAlQura.Astronomical.first_day_of_month/2`: a month's first day.

Two answers are found from the kept values rather than by keeping their own, since they are asked of a day: a lunisolar day's new year and month start, and an observational day's month. The day is placed between kept new years and month starts from an estimate of its year or month, and worked out as before where the search does not settle or a value is not found, so a day outside the ephemeris raises as it did. A computation that raises or answers an error keeps nothing. Without the application started the table is absent and every value is worked out. The table is cleared at 100,000 entries.

### Measured

On Astro 2.9.0 with the bundled kernel, Elixir 1.20.4 / OTP 29, µs a call. Cold is the first question about a year, the cache cleared before it; warm a later one.

| Persian `date_from_iso_days/1` | 77 | 413 | 237 | 183 | 1.0 |
| Chinese `days_in_month/2` | 262 | 664 | 595 | 610 | 1.7 |
| Chinese `date_to_iso_days/3` | 92 | 255 | 222 | 231 | 0.8 |

| `Date.shift(date, month: 1)`, Chinese | | | 4,460 | 778 | 20 |
| Observational `date_to_iso_days/3` | — | 16,700 | 11,580 | 11,650 | 0.05 |

| Astronomical Umm al-Qura month | — | 5,850 | 4,150 | 4,530 | 0.1 |

The observational calendars cannot run on Astro 2.7.0, which has no coverage check. A cold lunisolar `date_from_iso_days/1` works out the day's year, its twelfth month's start and the month or two around the day, about a fifth more than before; every later day of the year is a lookup. A cold observational day finds only its own month where it is fewer than 29 days into it, every month being 29 or 30 days, and the next month too otherwise; a day whose own crescent search would leave the ephemeris is sought as before, so it raises as it did.

### Verified

Every answer is the same with and without the cache, compared on Astro `main`: 496,602 answers (every day of Gregorian 1900 to 2100 for the Persian and lunisolar calendars, every 997th day from ISO day -1,000,000 to 2,000,000, every year's leap year, months, month lengths, month starts and validity for 1899 to 2101 and every 37th year of eight millennia, Persian years, every astronomical Umm al-Qura month); 547,860 far days (every day of 1000 to 901 BC, AD 1000 to 1099 and 2500 to 2599); 35,100 solar terms; and both observational calendars' every day of 1900 to 2100 and every month of 1316 to 1527 AH. `test/year_cache_calendars_test.exs` holds the cached answers to fresh ones and a day's date to the new year, month start and crescent found from the day itself.

## Open questions

* None for the cache. The tables' questions (whether to fix the observational months by a table, and over which span) no longer arise: the cache keeps what the installed ephemeris and Astro give.
